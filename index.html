Albert J. Lee

Ph.D. candidate, Machine Learning · Georgia Tech

I work on understanding and correcting the behavior of language models: finding where a behavior comes from inside the model, changing it, and checking that the change does what it appears to and nothing else. My Ph.D. studies failures such as deferring to a confident but wrong suggestion, a sycophancy-like behavior, in medical LLMs and VLMs, where a wrong answer has a real cost.

My tools are causal interventions on model internals (activation patching, steering, probing), targeted post-training (SFT and RL with verifiable rewards), and counterfactual evaluations that measure side effects as well as the intended fix. A recurring theme is skepticism about my own tools: I test whether an interpretability result or a fix really means what it seems to.

I'm finishing a Ph.D. in Machine Learning at Georgia Tech (expected December 2026), advised by Cassie S. Mitchell, after training in neuroscience. Before and alongside this I've worked on ML for biology and medicine, including a 2026 internship at LG AI Research, and most recently humanoid robot learning. I've changed fields a few times, and learning a new one from the ground up is the part I enjoy most.

Research

  • 2026
    What fine-tuning against wrong suggestions gains and loses

    Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to resist wrong diagnostic suggestions (KL-regularized LoRA on the gold answer, compared with GRPO) raises accuracy under wrong suggestions (30.5% → 71.5%) but lowers it under correct ones (89.1% → 62.2%). What the model lost was only its bias toward following suggestions; its ability to tell right suggestions from wrong ones went up (AUROC 0.684 → 0.741). Cross-model activation patching puts the two changes at different tokens, the bias at the suggestion and the discrimination gain at the answer options, so steering just the suggestion tokens recovers 59% of the loss while keeping 79% of the gain, also on held-out pairs.

    ACL ARR 2026 submission
  • 2025 – 2026
    Whether interpretability tools mean what they seem to

    One activation direction reduces cue-following across seven kinds of answer-suggesting cues in three LLM families, and comparing it with cue-specific and random directions separates what is detectable from what works as an intervention. In VLMs, activation patching that repairs an answer flipped by an authoritative wrong prompt need not restore evidence from the image: donors from a different case recover as much as the same case, across 5 models.

    ACL ARR 2026 submission · AAAI 2027 submission
  • 2026 –
    Applying rules without being swayed by irrelevant goals

    Counterfactual evaluations of treatment-eligibility judgments, which should change when the rules change but not when only the patient's stated goal changes. I trace the rule-application failure to about 2% of attention heads and post-train only those heads with SFT and RL with verifiable rewards, against random-head and full-parameter baselines, measuring both the correction and its side effects.

  • 2026
    Causal discovery from single-cell perturbation data

    At LG AI Research I developed DCCD-J, a differentiable method that recovers causal graphs from interventional data with latent confounders and feedback loops. Replacing the prior method's alternating covariance estimation, which collapses on real data, with a jointly optimized Cholesky parameterization gave the best held-out likelihood on all 5 benchmarks and confounder-recovery F1 of 0.93 versus 0.23 under dense confounding. I also used attribution from single-cell foundation models (scFoundation, scLong) to choose the gene set.

    NeurIPS 2026 AI4DD workshop (co-first author)
  • 2022 – 2026
    Building ontology structure into representations

    HiCo-EL builds an ontology's type hierarchy into bi-encoder entity linking: +3.7 R@1 on MedMentions and +9.6 on diseases unseen in training. It is the linking step in the lab's text-to-knowledge tools, between mention extraction (CCS Explorer, TrialSieve) and reasoning over a UMLS concept graph (SemNet Explorer), which I also contributed to.
