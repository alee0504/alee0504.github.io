Albert J. Lee

Research Intern · PIA Robotics  /  Ph.D. candidate, Machine Learning · Georgia Tech

I work on robot learning for humanoids. In August 2026 I joined PIA Robotics, a pre-seed humanoid startup, with no prior robotics experience, and built its sim-to-real pipeline for whole-body control from scratch: policies are trained with RL in Isaac Lab, checked in MuJoCo, and deployed on a Unitree G1. I also built a pipeline that turns text-generated human motion into robot reference motion through learned point-cloud correspondence between the human and robot bodies, trained BeyondMimic motion-tracking policies on it, and deployed them on the G1.

I'm finishing a Ph.D. in Machine Learning at Georgia Tech (expected May 2027), advised by Cassie S. Mitchell. The thread through my work is how behavior gets produced. I studied it in brains as a neuroscience undergraduate, then inside LLMs and VLMs during my Ph.D., using causal interventions (activation patching, steering, targeted post-training) to find where specific behaviors come from. Now I study it in robot bodies. Each move has meant learning a new field from the ground up, and that is the part I enjoy most.

  • PIA · 2026
    From text to robot motion: correspondence-based retargeting and motion tracking

    Human motion is generated from text with Kimodo on the SOMA body model, skinned into a human body mesh, and retargeted to the G1 through point-cloud correspondence between the human and robot body surfaces, learned with a PointNet autoencoder, under contact, joint-limit, and floor constraints, rather than through skeleton-based joint mapping. I trained BeyondMimic motion-tracking policies on the retargeted references and deployed them on the robot.

Ph.D. research

Most of my Ph.D. asked where a model's behavior comes from and whether changing it does what we think. Earlier work built structure into representations and modeled disease dynamics.

  • 2026
    What post-training changes inside a model

    Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (KL-regularized LoRA on the gold answer, compared with GRPO) to resist wrong diagnostic suggestions raises accuracy under wrong suggestions (30.5% → 71.5%) but lowers it under correct ones (89.1% → 62.2%). What it lost was only its bias toward following suggestions; its ability to tell right suggestions from wrong ones went up (AUROC 0.684 → 0.741). The two changes sit at different tokens, the bias at the suggestion and the discrimination gain at the answer options, so steering just the suggestion tokens recovers 59% of the loss while keeping 79% of the gain, also on held-out pairs. In ongoing work, I trace a rule-application failure to about 2% of attention heads and post-train only those heads with SFT and RL with verifiable rewards.

    ACL ARR 2026 submission · ongoing
  • 2025 – 2026
    Whether interpretability tools mean what they seem to

    One activation direction reduces cue-following across seven kinds of answer-suggesting cues in three LLM families, and comparing it with cue-specific and random directions separates what is detectable from what works as an intervention. In VLMs, activation patching that repairs an answer need not restore evidence from the image: donors from a different case recover as much as the same case.

    ACL ARR 2026 submission · AAAI 2027 submission
  • 2022 – 2026
    Building ontology structure into representations

    HiCo-EL builds an ontology's type hierarchy into bi-encoder entity linking: +3.7 R@1 on MedMentions and +9.6 on diseases unseen in training. It is the linking step in the lab's text-to-knowledge tools, between mention extraction (CCS Explorer, TrialSieve) and reasoning over a UMLS concept graph (SemNet Explorer), which I also contributed to.

    EMNLP 2026 (first author) · IEEE BigData 2022 · Bioengineering 2025 · BDCC 2026
  • 2016 – 2025
